# Karachi Planting Guide (Review Report)

Source: plain-language version of the Karachi Plant Species and Environmental Data Framework, 29 September 2026. Provided by the project supervisor.

## 1. The one thing to understand: salt decides what grows
In Karachi, salt decides what grows, not heat. The city never gets cold, so no plant dies of frost, and almost every plant can handle the temperature. What actually kills plants here is salt in the soil, salt in the water, poor drainage and bad irrigation water. The system must therefore check salt first and everything else after. Most plant apps in the world do it the other way round, which is why they do not work in Karachi.

## 2. What Karachi is like for plants
- **Rainfall:** about 310 mm a year, and 77% of it falls in July–August. Every planting needs irrigation.
- **Temperature:** normally 13 °C to 35 °C. It never freezes. There are two hot peaks, in May and in October.
- **Record heat:** 47.8 °C. Heat waves happen; the 2015 heat wave killed over 1,200 people.
- **Humidity:** 45% in winter and 70% in the monsoon. High humidity causes fungus and disease on vegetables.
- **Soil:** sandy, pH 7.4–7.9, i.e. alkaline, not acidic.
- **Lime in soil:** 13% to 31%. The soil is very chalky, which blocks iron and zinc, so leaves turn yellow.
- **Salt-affected land in Sindh:** 2.6 million hectares; nearly half of the surveyed land in Sindh has a salt problem.
- **Green cover in Karachi:** about 1%, against a recommended minimum of 25%, so there is a lot of room to plant.

## 3. Four kinds of sites
Every location falls into one of four site types, and each has a different main problem.
- **Coastal** (Hawke's Bay, Clifton, Korangi Creek, Ibrahim Hyderi). Main problem: salt in the soil and salt in the sea air. What works: mangrove, peelu, farash, Pongamia, Casuarina.
- **Dense urban** (Saddar, Lyari, Gulshan, road medians). Main problem: no root space, heat and pollution. What works: tough shade trees, or pots if space is small.
- **Peri-urban farm** (Gadap, Malir, Memon Goth). Main problem: irrigation water quality. What works: vegetables, fruit trees and fodder.
- **Inland dry land** (Kirthar foothills, open Gadap). Main problem: not enough water. What works: native desert trees and range grasses.

## 4. Six things that kill plants in Karachi
1. **Salt in the soil.** This is the biggest one. It is measured as ECe; above 4 dS/m most normal vegetables start losing yield.
2. **Sodic soil.** This is different from salty soil: the soil goes hard and water will not soak in. It needs gypsum, not just washing. Washing a sodic soil without gypsum makes it worse.
3. **Bad irrigation water.** This is the one everybody misses. Water can pass a normal salt test and still ruin the soil. The hidden number is RSC; above 2.5 the water is not fit for irrigation.
4. **No drainage.** If water cannot drain away, salt builds up instead of washing out. A well-drained salty site is better than a badly drained, slightly salty one.
5. **No root space.** A tree in a 1.2 m² pavement hole will die no matter which species is picked. Check space before checking anything else.
6. **Not enough watering at the start.** Most failures are this. A tree that needs three years of watering will die with someone who waters twice a week, whatever its salt tolerance.

## 5. What we need to know about a site (8 questions)
The input form needs nothing more than these questions:
1. Where is it? (drop a pin on the map)
2. How big is the area, and is it ground, a raised bed or a pot?
3. How deep can roots go before hitting rock, rubble or a slab?
4. How many hours of direct sun does it get?
5. Where does the water come from, and how many days a week can you water?
6. What do you want: food, shade, screening, flowers, habitat or fodder?
7. How much care can you give: daily, a few times a week, or rarely?
8. Do you have a soil test?

If there is no soil test, three simple questions are asked instead: Is there a white crust on the dry soil? What grows there already without watering? Has anything died after seeming to settle in? These answers give a rough guess only, never a confident answer.

## 6. What to plant
The full report covers about 65 plants. Short version by group:
- **Vegetables and field crops.** Good choices: barley, cotton, sorghum, guar, wheat, cowpea, beetroot, zucchini, bermuda grass. Risky: tomato, cucumber, spinach, cabbage, okra, chilli, brinjal. Avoid on salty soil: lettuce, onion, beans.
- **Fruit trees.** Good choices: date palm, guava, chikoo, ber, pomegranate, jamun. Need good soil and water: banana, mango, coconut, papaya. Poor in Karachi: orange, grape.
- **Shade and street trees.** Good choices: neem, Pongamia (sukhchain), babul, kandi, siris, arjun, jangli badam, amaltas, Casuarina. Only with big root space: peepal, banyan. Do not plant: Conocarpus, gulmohar.
- **Coastal and salty sites.** Good choices: mangrove (tidal areas only), peelu, farash, Cordia, kirir, saltbush, moringa. Prosopis juliflora works but spreads and becomes a weed.
- **Flowers and ground cover.** Good choices: bougainvillea, Dodonaea, henna, jasmine, aloe vera, bermuda grass. Oleander is poisonous and must not be planted where children or animals can reach it.

**On Conocarpus:** it makes up 62% of Karachi's street trees and was banned in 2016, and KMC is already replacing it with neem. It damages pipes and drains, uses a lot of water and causes pollen allergy, so the system must not recommend it.

## 7. How the system should decide
Three simple steps, in this order:
1. **Remove what cannot survive.** Plants that are too salt-sensitive for the site, need more root space than there is, are poisonous where children play, or need more watering than the user can give are thrown out completely. A good score elsewhere cannot save them.
2. **Score what is left, out of 100.** Salt gets the most weight (22%), then water need (18%), then temperature (14%), then soil, space, care, purpose, lime and wind.
3. **Say how sure we are.** High means lab results are available, Medium means partly tested, and Low means the answer is a guess from the type of site. At Low confidence no number is shown, only the test that would fix it.

Always tell the user why: both why a plant was chosen and why one was rejected, with the actual number that caused it. Farmers and agronomists check the rejections first.

## 8. Three worked examples
These examples use made-up site values to show how the system works; they are not measurements from real places.
- **A. Coastal plot near Hawke's Bay.** The soil is very salty (ECe 11.4) and water comes by tanker twice a week. Almost all vegetables and fruit trees are out. Recommended: peelu, Pongamia and farash, with a Casuarina windbreak on the sea side. The real risk is not salt but finding enough water for the first three years.
- **B. Tree pit in Saddar.** A 1.2 m² hole in the pavement with concrete 60 cm down and no soil test. Peepal, banyan and gulmohar are rejected straight away because the hole is too small; no test is needed for that. Neem and Pongamia are possible, but nobody can be sure without a soil test. Honest answer: make the hole bigger first, then test the soil.
- **C. Farm in Gadap.** The soil is moderately salty and the borewell water has an RSC of 3.1, above the safe limit. Date palm, guava, sorghum, cowpea and beetroot all work well. Tomato will lose about a quarter of its yield. Onion, orange and beans are out. The real advice is to fix the water first with gypsum and add extra water for leaching; otherwise, within a few seasons, even the good crops will start failing.

## 9. Tests we should get done
Two cheap test panels turn a guess into a real answer.

**Minimum soil test:**
- ECe (salt level): the single most important number; nothing works without it.
- pH: shows lime and sodic problems.
- Texture (sand, silt or clay): affects drainage and watering.
- SAR or sodium: shows whether washing the soil will work or make it worse.
- Lime (CaCO3): explains yellow leaves and locked-up nutrients.
- Organic matter: soil health and water holding.

**Minimum water test:**
- EC and pH: basic salt level of the water.
- Sodium, calcium and magnesium: needed to calculate SAR.
- Carbonate and bicarbonate: needed to calculate RSC, the number everyone forgets.
- Chloride and boron: cause leaf burn and toxicity.
- Heavy metals: only if the water comes from a drain or wastewater.

**How to sample:** take 8–12 small scoops across the plot and mix them, and take two depths separately (0–30 cm and 30–60 cm). Sample at the end of the dry season, before the monsoon, when salt is at its highest. Do not mix a salty patch with clean soil; test them separately.

## 10. What we still do not know
- **Okra:** a major Karachi crop, with no published salt limit anywhere.
- **Chikoo:** Karachi's biggest fruit crop by area (302 hectares), with no salt data at all.
- **Conocarpus:** millions were planted because it is "salt tolerant", but nobody has ever published a number for it.
- **Vegetable planting dates for lower Sindh:** no official Sindh calendar exists in public, so national dates are used as a rough guide.
- **Karachi groundwater salt levels:** known to be brackish, but there are no reliable published figures.
- **Air quality:** Karachi has no working public monitoring station, so pollution cannot be used as a hard rule, only as advice.

None of these are guessed at in the system; they are shown as gaps, together with the test that would fill them.

## 11. The plan
- **Phase 1 (weeks 1–6):** the 8 questions, the pass/fail rules, the first plant list and clear reasons for rejection. Before moving on, every rejection must name its rule and its source.
- **Phase 2 (weeks 7–14):** scoring out of 100, confidence levels, and weather and soil data feeds. Before moving on, no score may be shown when the data is only a guess.
- **Phase 3 (weeks 15–26):** soil and water test ordering, uploading results and unlocking full confidence. Target: 50 sites with real lab results.
- **Phase 4 (month 7 onwards):** follow-up at 3, 12 and 36 months, learning from what lived and what died.

Phase 1 is the one that has to be right. It needs no weather data, no scoring engine and no laboratory, and it already gives a recommendation that an expert can check and trust.

## 12. Bottom line
Check salt first. Be honest when we do not know. Get people to test their soil. If we do those three things, the system will be more useful and more trusted than anything else available for Karachi.
